Assessment

Why are we assessing our courses?

Our college follows a Comprehensive Assessment Plan for Institutional Effectiveness (CAPIE).

A culture of continuous assessment is also expected by accreditation bodies.

We are following a 5-year Plan of Assessment (Fall 2026 - Spring 2031) for our discipline/program. This plan is prepared by the faculty in order to assess our Program Learning Outcomes (PLOs). PLOs are also prepared by the faculty, discussed and voted by the department, approved by College Curriculum committee(s) and Governance bodies.

Where does the assessment data go?

Our assessment data is used to write an internal annual report that must be shared with faculty and sent to the Associate Vice President for Academic Affairs.

This is a copy of the annual assessment report form (*.docx file).

These are our assessment reports for Astronomy: 2025, 2026

According to our institution's Internal Review Board (IRB) policies, this internal assessment data does not require approval from IRB as long as the data remains internal to the institution. If faculty is ever interested in publishing any of these results in the future, this action will require special IRB approval.

Types of assessment tools

There is no assessment instrument that can test all PLOs at the same time. Thus, we will always use several different forms of assessment.

As described in the current 5-year Plan for Astronomy, some PLOs will be assessed inside physics classes with instruments chosen by physics faculty. Once agreed upon, assessment tools must be the same for all faculty, without modifications. Examples of possible assessment tools: embedded questions, classroom presentations, laboratory reports, written projects, standardized national exams, final exams, etc.

It is always interesting to use at least one instrument that can be compared with results in the literature. Astronomy Education Research is often combined and/or housed within Physics Education Research (PER), and this is a link to an important PER databased (for literature search): https://www.per-central.org/.

The best link to find nationally standardized (research-based) assessment tools for astronomy is https://www.physport.org/ .

Protecting assessment tools

If you are a faculty member planning to use a standardized assessment instrument, you should register in https://www.physport.org/ and download the tool yourself because you will be prompted to read several security messages about protecting the assessment tools and how to use them. It's a good idea to be aware of it all.

The answer keys are also available in PhysPort.org (click on the Assessment tab, choose research-based assessments, then search for Astronomy assessments).

Motivating students to participate in the assessment by reading and answering all questions honestly: faculty, your speech is very important

Tell your students the assessment (it's a good idea to call it a survey, rather than test or even assessment) is designed to evaluate the course (not them), and that knowing how they think will help you teach better. Tell them that correctness will not affect their grades (only participation) to help alleviate students anxiety.

Ask students to read all question and answer them honestly, leaving NO BLANKS. If a topic is completely new, ask students to choose their best guess, but to never leave any questions unanswered, as this will be important in the final analysis.

Explain to students that they will see the same survey at the end of the semester, so that we can measure how much they have learned/absorbed during the semester. Remember to always collect the survey: students cannot take the survey home or download it.

Suggestion: you could offer an extra credit point for participation in the assessment.

From the point of view of data analysis, it is very important that students read and answer all questions honestly, instead of skipping, guessing or rushing through the whole survey. The factor that influences this the most is the speech given by the instructor, to impart the correct importance to this survey.
